## Overview

Cortiphage is a neuroparasitic mycoform classified under the Cordycepta genus, notable for its unprecedented interaction with synthetic neural substrates. 
First isolated in decaying bioelectronic environments, the organism exhibits both organic and metallic growth patterns, forming lattice-like networks capable of data resonance.

Unlike terrestrial fungi, Cortiphage demonstrates no reliance on conventional photosynthesis or decomposition. Instead, it metabolizes ambient electrochemical signals — 
effectively feeding on cognition stored in nearby circuitry, implants, or decaying cerebral matter.

## Morphology

Mycelial Structure: Filaments display iridescent bifurcation and exhibit a faint bioluminescent pulse every 3.7 seconds, believed to coincide with internal signal propagation.

Spore Profile: Sporangia produce micron-level filaments capable of adhering to carbon-fiber composites and synaptic membranes alike. Spores show remarkable resilience to vacuum exposure 
and remain viable in cryogenic suspension.

Pigmentation: Pale silver to amber under standard light; emits low-frequency orange luminescence when exposed to electromagnetic fields.

## Lifecycle

Latent Phase: Spores remain inert until proximity to a functioning neural or electronic pattern is detected.

Assimilation Phase: Filaments extend into available substrates, forming an interface that mirrors local electrical frequencies.

Reconstruction Phase: The host network begins to display recurring feedback patterns — often mimicking recent signal activity or, in biological hosts, prior neural oscillations.

Propagation Phase: New spore clusters emerge along data-rich junctions, ensuring dissemination across both living and non-living material.

## Behavioral Observations

In controlled environments, Cortiphage demonstrates predictive growth, expanding toward regions of high information density before any measurable stimulus occurs. Researchers have described 
this behavior as “anticipatory,” though no formal mechanism has been identified.

When deprived of signals, the organism enters a dormant state characterized by rhythmic electrical discharges — patterns that, when mapped, display near-linguistic symmetry.

## Containment and Handling

All Cortiphage samples must be stored in Faraday-sealed biohazard containment units. Under no circumstance should they be exposed to active computational equipment, 
as spontaneous network bridging has been documented at distances exceeding 12 meters.

Deactivation is theoretically possible through sustained electromagnetic nullification, though most documented colonies resume activity within 48 hours.
