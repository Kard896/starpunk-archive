## Overview

Glasvine is an inert climbing plant with no known medicinal, nutritional, or industrial value. Its semi-transparent stems refract light slightly, giving an illusion of vibrancy under certain angles — a trait that has led to its repeated misclassification as something interesting.

The plant produces no scent, no fruit, and no detectable bioactive compounds. Genetic sequencing reveals near-perfect redundancy in its DNA, with several million base pairs devoted to non-coding repetition.

## Ecological Role

None of significance. Glasvine tends to grow on the edges of other flora, particularly those worth studying. Its presence is considered a mild nuisance during survey operations, as its fibers are prone to tangling in drone rotors and data cables.

## Cultural Notes

In some frontier settlements, “Glasvine” is used as a colloquial term for something that looks valuable but isn’t.
Despite several funding attempts, no one has found a reason to study it twice.
