## Overview

Lumestra is a photosynthetic vascular species native to subpolar biomes, renowned for its bio-reactive luminescence and regenerative properties. Its sap contains a compound known as Luminase, capable of stabilizing cellular degradation and stimulating rapid tissue repair in most known carbon-based lifeforms.

The plant is a paradox of fragility and resilience — its translucent fronds collapse at a whisper, yet the organism can regrow an entire stalk from a single cell fragment within hours of exposure to ultraviolet light.

## Applications

Polaris Systems classifies Lumestra as a Class-2 Biopharmaceutical Resource, harvested for synthesis of LumeGel™, a medical compound used to treat radiation burns and neural shock. Controlled trials indicate up to 90% efficiency in restoring synaptic coherence after trauma.

Unofficial reports claim that extended use induces mild phototropic hallucinations — patients describe “seeing the air breathe” or “hearing warmth.”
These effects remain unconfirmed and, according to Polaris documentation, “statistically insignificant.”

## Containment and Cultivation

Requires narrowband UV exposure and a soil substrate rich in trace silicates. When deprived of light, Lumestra folds into itself, entering a chrysalis-like dormancy during which its luminescence dims but never fully ceases.
